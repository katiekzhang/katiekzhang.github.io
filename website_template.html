Pollution Without Borders: Regulatory Displacement in South Asian Manufacturing

Sole-authored · Draft November 2025

When environmental standards tighten in one jurisdiction, polluting activity often relocates rather than disappears. Using a panel of 240 industrial districts across India and Bangladesh and a regression-discontinuity design around the 2018 Indian Air Quality Standards revision, I show that emissions-intensive firms — particularly brick kilns and textile dyeing operations — systematically reorganize across the border in response to enforcement.

Headline Tightening Indian standards causes a +12.4% increase in PM2.5 in adjacent Bangladeshi districts within 18 months, fully offsetting the domestic improvement at the regional level.

Satellite Monitoring and Firm Compliance: Evidence from a Randomized Disclosure Policy
with A. Kumar · R&R, AEJ: Applied · 2024

We randomize whether firms are informed that their emissions are being tracked via satellite. Treated firms reduce visible pollution by 18% but shift toward less detectable pollutants — suggesting monitoring technologies create predictable evasion margins.
